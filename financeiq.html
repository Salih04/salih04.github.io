FinanceIQ: an evaluation harness that audited its own result

Can one year's public data about a Borsa Istanbul company rank stocks by next year's return? The harness says no. The more useful part is how it got there: frozen data rejected, every estimate reported with its null and its power, and a late audit that removed the one positive number it had published.

02 · method

Walk-forward, with the null attached

Features of year T predict the return of year T+1; each test year (2023, 2024, 2025) is predicted only by models trained on earlier target years. The metric is the within-year Spearman rank correlation (IC), pooled over test years and compared with a null built by permuting returns within each year. Six ML models are corrected for multiplicity; three rank baselines are reported beside them.

03 · audit

The number that did not survive

The published evaluation had one nominally significant result: an equal-weight baseline with pooled IC +0.150 (p = 0.017). An audit in October 2026 asked when each feature could have been known. Year-T fundamentals turned out to be the full fiscal-year statements — matching published annual figures to the million lira — and those are filed weeks into the year whose return they predict.

The published results were kept byte-for-byte; the corrected analysis sits beside them with a script that refuses to run unless it first reproduces the published leaderboard. The audit also found share counts multiplied by back-adjusted prices, and a forward pre-registration frozen inside its own outcome window. Both are documented, not hidden.

04 · reading

What the result means

  • supported
    No model ranks next-year returns better than chance after correction, and the one positive baseline number is explained by information that was not yet public.
  • power
    The design could detect only large effects (|IC| ≳ 0.18–0.4). A null here means no large edge, not no edge.
  • scope
    One high-inflation period in nominal lira, a retrospectively chosen universe, no survivorship data.
  • not claimed
    Market efficiency, useless fundamentals, or that a better model would fail.
